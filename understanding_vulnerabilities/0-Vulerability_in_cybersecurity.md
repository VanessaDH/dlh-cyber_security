** 1. Introduction to Vulnerabilities**
_ What is a vulnerability?
  A weak spot in a system that someone could use to break in, steal data, or cause damage.
_ Why is it more than just a technical weakness?
  Because a flaw alone isn't dangerous — it only becomes a real problem once someone figures out how to use it against you. It's a mix of a mistake   and bad intent.
_ How do vulnerabilities lead to breaches?
  Attackers find a weak spot and use it to get in. Find the flaw + exploit it = breach. That's basically how most hacks happen.
<br>
**2. Historical context, in short:**
     Early on, software flaws were seen as harmless glitches since computers weren't connected to much. That changed once networking took off —
     the 1988 Morris Worm showed how one small flaw could spread and cause widespread damage. As the internet grew and attackers got smarter,            vulnerabilities became a real threat: stolen data, broken systems, financial loss. By the late 1990s, the industry even built a shared
     tracking system (CVE) just to keep up. Today, vulnerabilities are treated as a serious business risk, not a minor technical issue.
<br>
**3.Types of Vulnerabilities Explained:**
_ Different types of vulnerabilities matter because they each open a different door for attackers. A company can have perfectly secure code but still get breached through a network mistake or a tricked employee. So real security means covering every type — software, hardware, network, and human — not just one. Missing any single one can undo all the work put into the others.
_ Software vulnerabilities: bugs or flaws in code, like something that lets someone bypass a login. The most common type.

Hardware vulnerabilities: flaws in the physical chip itself, like Spectre and Meltdown (2018). Hard to fix since you can't just send a quick update.

Network vulnerabilities: setup mistakes, like an open port or a weak firewall rule. Lets attackers in without touching any code.

Human vulnerabilities: people-based issues, like phishing emails or an insider misusing access. Often the easiest to exploit — one careless click can undo everything else.
